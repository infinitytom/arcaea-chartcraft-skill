# Phrase design, hand routes and review

Use this for creating a chart or substantially revising its musical and spatial
organization. These are decision criteria, not a required pattern bank.

## Musical evidence and phrase roles

Choose the perceptually defining layer: melody, vocal phrase, characteristic
rhythm, sound-design attack or structural accent. Repeated kicks are not always
the defining layer. High RMS in a bass stem does not establish musical priority.
Use stems or pitch candidates when they resolve a real ambiguity; check leakage,
confidence, durations, onset latency and grid error. Do not turn every model note
or transient into a judgement object.

Maintain a section brief with each phrase's time range, selected layer, anchors,
vocabulary, pressure, intended hands and recovery. Mark automatic section labels
as hypotheses. Deliberately choose whether a boundary attacks, transforms or
releases. If new analysis contradicts a planned explosion, revise the brief
instead of filling weakly supported positions to satisfy a density budget.

Use a grid appropriate to the recording. Phrase control points need rhythmic or
musical justification; dividing every Arc into equal thirds can manufacture
arbitrary turns. Off-grid timing can be correct for expressive recordings or
supported local events. Do not enforce a universal onset-alignment percentage.

## Development rather than mechanical repetition

Identify a song-specific gesture idea. Introduce it readably, develop it with
the orchestration and leave a meaningful exit. Development may change the layer,
object type, hand ownership, contact height, direction, continuous control or
release while retaining recognizable musical anchors.

Avoid an every-N-bars short-Arc schedule with floor taps filling the remainder.
Mirroring that schedule, randomly changing a curve, adding a larger sweep or
raising the difficulty label does not repair the underlying phrase structure.
Long snakes are one option, not a mandatory duration target. Short cuts,
stationary anchors, sky streams and floor streams can also be intentional.

Build contrast in tasks, not only counts: a continuous Arc phrase, two-hand
skyline, floor/sky relay, compound accent, rotating paired gesture, sparse hold
and recovery can express different roles. Do not force every listed configuration
into a song. Decorative traces must not hide a thin playable arrangement.

Exact or approximate repeat signatures can locate possible mechanical passages.
Compare timing, object types, intended hands and spatial routes, including mirrored
versions. Musical repetition is valid; no exact repeats is not proof of novelty.
Likewise high movement, dense controls or an elaborate visual theme is not proof
of beauty. Check coherence, visual hierarchy, contact separation and the phrase's
relationship to the music.

## Tension and cross-field reaches

Here, tension concerns the gesture's timing: excursion over a meaningful interval,
acceleration, braking, direction changes, sustained control and the other hand's
task. Use prepared large movements, high anchors with cross-field responses,
opposed directions, carefully separated paired motion, and deliberate release
where appropriate. Do not measure it solely by x span, velocity or segment count.

A cross-field reach (出张) can occur when one hand follows an Arc and the free
hand reaches to floor or sky contacts across its usual half. Arc color does not
forbid that Arc from crossing the field, and lane 1/2 does not require the left
hand. Document an intended solution over the entire sequence, including the
approach and return. Color-based hand assignment is itself an assumption; record
planned swaps or alternative solutions explicitly when needed.

For a hand plan, useful fields are:

- Sustains: intended hand, kind, start/end, exact Arc control points/times or hold
  lane, and phrase role.
- Instant inputs: chart event identity/time, intended hand, floor lane or computed
  sky x/y, musical evidence and role.

Audit against the parsed AFF, not just against reservations in the generator.
Confirm that the plan covers the actual objects and does not assign independent
new inputs to an occupied hand. Internal Arc seams remain continuously occupied.
Pool the full exact-endpoint ±1 ms candidate neighborhood for branch decisions.

Compute sky positions using the parent Trace's actual easing. For rapid travel,
track the last actual contact including Arc/hold endpoints, plus the next sustain
head. A planner that remembers only previous taps can choose a locally cheap but
physically poor recovery after a full-field Arc. Check free-hand floor/sky layer
changes during an active Arc: repeated screen-diagonal jumps in one short
subdivision may be accidental rather than expressive pressure.

Possible repairs include changing which hand owns the next contact, carrying a
short phrase on one layer, preparing the destination, reducing a secondary
accent or changing the exit. Do not apply one permanent per-hand half-screen lock
or globally suppress sky notes under input Arcs.

## Difficulty and independent review

Count playable objects separately from new gestures. A continuous same-color Arc
can have many segments while remaining one chain start. Trace decoration is not
playable density, and neither count is the game's combo/tick total.

When a chart feels too simple, inspect combined coordination, rhythmic detail,
sustained pressure, spatial decisions and recovery in the relevant musical
section. Use official references conditioned on style, phrase role and target
difficulty for context; do not copy event sequences or fit a single official
average. Do not infer an exact rating from density or claim a selected FTR 10
label is validated.

Keep these evidence levels separate:

- Signal analysis/transcription: uncertain musical candidates.
- AFF/asset checks: format and project completeness.
- Hand/contact analysis: a declared route and coordinate heuristics.
- Render inspection: what was actually rendered and viewed.
- Listening review: audio-to-chart correspondence actually checked by listening.
- Device playtesting: real readability, motion and technique feedback.

Heuristic warnings are review prompts. Official difficult charts also contain
rapid recovery, reversals and contact proximity. Clearing every warning can erase
legitimate cross-hand play; an official precedent does not prove a new pattern
feels fair. Local spacing/time thresholds must be described as proxies, not
published lowiro judgement rules.
